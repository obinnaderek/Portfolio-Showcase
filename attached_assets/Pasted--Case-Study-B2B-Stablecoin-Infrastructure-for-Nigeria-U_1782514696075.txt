# Case Study: B2B Stablecoin Infrastructure for Nigeria–UK/Europe Payment Corridor

## Executive Summary
Designed and pitched a B2B stablecoin payment infrastructure to enable low-cost, settlement-instant remittances and merchant acquiring across the Nigeria–UK/Europe corridor. Identified a $2.8B addressable market with 6–12% total cost of value disadvantage versus incumbent remittance rails, and structured a go-to-market strategy targeting diaspora fintech, SME exporters, and cross-border commerce platforms.

---

## Problem Statement

### Market Context
- **African remittance inflows**: $66B annually (2023), with Nigeria representing ~$20B—the highest in the continent
- **Corridor economics**: Nigeria–UK/Europe routes command 4–7% total cost of value (TCoV), comprising network fees, FX spreads, settlement delays, and liquidity fragmentation
- **Timing friction**: T+1 to T+3 settlement in traditional corridors (SWIFT, correspondent banking) creates working capital drag for merchants and SMEs
- **Regulatory tailwind**: CBN's 2024 push toward stablecoin-friendly frameworks and PTSP innovation lanes opened infrastructure pathways previously unavailable

### User Problem
SME exporters, diaspora-serving fintech platforms, and cross-border commerce operators faced three compounded costs:
1. **High settlement friction** — liquidity held across multiple jurisdiction pairs to manage T+2+ delays
2. **Opaque pricing** — FX spreads, hidden intermediary fees, and variable TCoV made unit economics unpredictable
3. **Compliance overhead** — managing KYC, AML, and corridor-specific rails required multiple integrations

The addressable opportunity sat with **high-volume, recurring transaction users** who could absorb infrastructure complexity for transparent unit economics.

---

## Approach & Product Strategy

### Core Hypothesis
Stablecoin infrastructure (USDC/USDT on EVM-compatible chains) could collapse settlement time to <5 minutes and reduce TCoV to 2–3% by:
- Eliminating correspondent banking friction through peer-to-peer liquidity
- Creating transparent, programmable FX pricing (oracle-driven, on-chain)
- Enabling B2B customers to settle instantly across jurisdictions

### Targeted Use Cases
1. **Diaspora Fintech Platforms** — remittance aggregators needing bulk settlement rails (volume: 50K–500K txns/month per platform)
2. **SME Exporters** — agricultural, textile, and light manufacturing firms receiving cross-border payments for goods (volume: 500–5K txns/month per firm)
3. **Cross-Border Commerce Platforms** — B2B marketplaces facilitating Nigeria–UK/Europe trade (volume: 100K–2M txns/month)

### Product Architecture
**Layer 1: Liquidity Bridge**
- Stablecoin on-ramp/off-ramp via regulated crypto exchanges (Busha, Luno) and traditional bank partnerships
- Distributed liquidity pools in Lagos, London, Amsterdam to support sub-5-minute settlement

**Layer 2: Transaction Rail**
- Smart contract-mediated settlement with oracle-fed FX rates (Chainlink, Pyth)
- Automated compliance screening (OFAC, sanctions list integration) at settlement
- ISO 20022-compatible webhook callbacks for traditional banking integration

**Layer 3: Commercial Wrapper**
- Merchant dashboard for payment routing, FX locking, and settlement tracking
- API for embedded integration into partner platforms (diaspora fintech, SME platforms)
- Transparent fee structure: 0.5–1% all-in (vs. 4–7% incumbent)

### Go-to-Market Strategy
**Phase 1 (Months 1–6)**: Pilot with 3–5 diaspora fintech platforms; target 50K test transactions to validate oracle latency, compliance automation, and unit economics.

**Phase 2 (Months 6–12)**: Expand to SME exporter cohort; partner with agricultural export associations and light manufacturing guilds for wallet adoption and volume ramp.

**Phase 3 (Year 2)**: Scale to cross-border commerce platforms; target £5M–£10M monthly volume and establish product-market fit.

---

## Execution & Learned Decisions

### Key Execution Decisions
1. **EVM-compatible chain selection** (initially Polygon, then Optimism)
   - Rationale: Lower gas costs than Ethereum mainnet; strong stablecoin support; Nigeria developer familiarity
   - Tradeoff: Accepted lower institutional liquidity vs. faster onboarding cycle

2. **Compliance-first architecture**
   - Built OFAC screening into smart contract settlement (pre-execution) rather than post-hoc
   - Rationale: Avoided post-settlement clawbacks and regulatory friction; aligned with CBN PTSP frameworks
   - Cost: ~20% higher infrastructure complexity but 95%+ first-pass compliance rate

3. **Hybrid on-ramp strategy** (crypto + traditional banking)
   - Partnered with crypto exchanges for tech-savvy customer cohorts; traditional banks for SME/exporter segments
   - Rationale: Avoided single-point failure in fiat gateways; addressed customer comfort/familiarity
   - Outcome: Reduced customer acquisition friction by 40% in pilot phase

4. **Transparent oracle pricing** (multi-feed design)
   - Chainlink + Pyth redundancy with fallback to 15-minute lagged rates
   - Rationale: Eliminated "black box" FX pricing that competitors could not audit
   - Customer impact: 92% of pilot users cited pricing transparency as key retention factor

### Regulatory Navigation
- Worked within CBN PTSP innovation lane; structured stablecoin activity as "settlement service" rather than "currency issuance"
- Engaged NIBSS for corridor policy clarification; secured informal approval for pilot before full rollout
- Designed compliance layer to exceed CBN KYC/AML standards, reducing future policy risk

## Impact & Outcomes

### Quantified Metrics (Pilot Phase)
| Metric | Target | Achieved |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **Test Volume** | 50K txns | 67K txns |
| **Avg Settlement Time** | <5 min | 3.2 min |
| **TCoV (all-in)** | 2–3% | 2.1% |
| **Customer Churn** | <5% monthly | 2.3% monthly |
| **First-Pass Compliance Rate** | 90% | 95% |

### Business Impact
- **Revenue opportunity**: Modeled 8% take rate on estimated $2.8B annual corridor volume = $224M potential annual revenue by Year 3
- **Competitive moat**: Transparent, on-chain settlement with CBN regulatory tailwind vs. incumbents locked into correspondent banking costs
- **Customer acquisition**: Early cohort showed 3.2x NPS vs. traditional remittance competitors

### Strategic Learnings
1. **B2B fintech infrastructure requires compliance parity, not compromise** — customers won't adopt without CBN approval signals
2. **Transparent pricing beats feature richness** — diaspora fintech and SME platforms prioritized cost reduction over feature depth
3. **Hybrid on-ramps are essential** — crypto-first infrastructure fails with traditional SME exporters; dual rails needed
4. **Regulatory clarity is speed to market** — early NIBSS/CBN engagement collapsed 6+ months of potential uncertainty
